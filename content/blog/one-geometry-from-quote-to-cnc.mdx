Ask a production manager where remakes come from and you'll hear the same
stories: the mitre that was on the quote but not the cut list, the cutout
that moved 40mm between revision B and revision C, the splashback that was
measured in millimetres and typed in centimetres.

These aren't carelessness. They're transcription - the same shape being
re-entered by different hands into systems that don't talk.

## The lifecycle of a shape

In a typical fabrication business, one benchtop's geometry gets created
several times:

| Stage | Where the shape lives | Who re-enters it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Enquiry | A sketch or a photo | The customer |
| Quote | The estimator's drawing or spreadsheet | The estimator |
| Order confirmation | A PDF the customer signs | Nobody - but nobody checks it either |
| Templating / final check | The templater's file | The templater |
| Production | CAD/CAM, nesting | A programmer |

Each hop is a chance for the shape to drift. Worse, when a revision lands
mid-stream, it has to be applied *in every copy* - and the copies have no
way of knowing they're stale.

## What "one geometry" means in practice

The alternative is structural, not procedural: the shape is captured once,
as data - not as a picture of data - and every later stage reads the same
record.

- **At quote time**, the drawing is the source of truth. Edges, cutouts,
  thicknesses and finishes are attributes of the geometry, not notes beside
  it.
- **At approval**, the customer signs off the actual record - so what was
  approved is what gets made, by definition.
- **At production**, the DXF and CNC-ready files are *exports of* the
  record, not re-drawings. If revision C moved the cutout, there is no
  revision B left anywhere to cut from.

This is the core architectural decision in Nyro: the quote canvas, the
order and the CNC export all read one geometry record. It's why a quote a
trade customer draws in the [Customer Portal](/customer) can land in a
factory as a cuttable file, and why the
[Fabrication Platform](/fabricator)'s floor tablets always show the current
shape.

## The payoff shows up in the remake bin

Fewer remakes is the visible win, but the quiet one is bigger:
**revisions get cheap.** When a change is one edit to one record, saying
"yes, we can move the sink" stops being an internal negotiation about who
re-draws what. The customer gets flexibility; the factory keeps certainty.

The shape is the job. Draw it once.
