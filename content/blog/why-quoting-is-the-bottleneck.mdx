Walk any benchtop factory and you'll find serious capacity: CNC routers,
edge polishers, people who can turn a slab into a finished top in hours.
Then walk into the front office and find the real constraint - a quoting
inbox that only one or two people can empty.

## The anatomy of a slow quote

A typical trade quote passes through more hands than the benchtop will:

1. **The enquiry arrives** as a phone call, an email, or a photo of a sketch
   on a piece of GIB offcut.
2. **Someone interprets it** - usually the estimator, who has to guess at
   edge details and confirm the sink model before anything can be priced.
3. **The geometry gets drawn** - once for the quote, and often again later
   for production, in a different system.
4. **The price gets calculated** against a rate sheet that lives in a
   spreadsheet, in someone's head, or both.
5. **The quote goes back** - and half the time the customer changes the
   shape, which starts the loop again.

None of these steps is slow on its own. The problem is that they queue
behind one person, and every revision re-enters the queue at the back.

## Why this caps revenue

Trade customers - kitchen companies, joiners, builders - quote *their*
customers based on *your* numbers. When your quote takes three days, their
quote takes four, and jobs drift to whoever answers fastest. You don't see
the jobs you lost this way; they never arrive as enquiries again.

The quoting queue also decides your mix. When estimating time is scarce, it
goes to the big, interesting jobs - and the steady bread-and-butter tops
that keep machines fed get quoted last, or lazily.

## What actually removes the bottleneck

Not templates, and not "we'll hire another estimator." The queue exists
because pricing knowledge lives in people. It disappears when three things
become software:

- **The shape is drawn once, by the person who knows the job.** Your
  customer draws the top - shape, cutouts, edges - and that drawing *is*
  the job, all the way to the machine.
- **Pricing rules run without a person.** Materials, machining, edgework,
  delivery, at each customer's own tier. If the rules can price 90% of
  jobs, the estimator only sees the weird 10%.
- **Revisions cost nothing.** When the customer moves the sink, the price
  updates in front of them. No email thread, no queue re-entry.

That's the design brief behind Nyro's [Customer Portal](/customer):
your customers quoting themselves on your catalog, at their own prices,
with the drawing flowing through to production untouched.

## The test

Count the quotes that left your office last week. Then count the ones a
customer could have done themselves if they'd had your catalog and their
prices in front of them. In most trade-heavy fabricators the second number
is well over half - and every one of them was queue time your estimator
spent transcribing instead of winning the hard jobs.
